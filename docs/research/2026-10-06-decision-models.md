# CLEF and JEV for EventEdge — decision memo

Reviewed 2026-10-06. Research only: no model calls, paid signups, package installs, strategy changes, ledger writes, or production actions occurred.

**Recommendation: defer production adoption; adopt a small shadow evaluation after the provider/data contracts are stable.** These models may improve inexpensive semantic screening, evidence checking, and selective escalation. They do not repair missing feeds, authenticate companies, enforce financial arithmetic, or establish profitable decisions. Start with Clef as a reproducible candidate and pinned Jev as a hosted comparator; treat Clef-flash as a speed/cost control. Do not replace the portfolio committee or deterministic risk controls yet.

## Exact identities and verified access

The paired names resolve to **Cloudflare Clef/Clef-flash** and **TypeSafe AI Jev**. Cloudflare's October 1 announcement explicitly compares the products; Jev was announced September 15. This is not the unrelated CLEF sequence-editing or EEG research. Both accept supplied state plus bounded questions and return typed choices/probabilities. Zero-shot use does not require a new training dataset. [Cloudflare announcement](https://blog.cloudflare.com/clef-decision-models/), [TypeSafe announcement](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev).

| Candidate | Verified current interface/access | Published input price | Reproducibility and local use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Clef | Workers AI `@cf/cloudflare/clef`; 27B; multimodal; 65,536-token hosted context; 1–64 questions | $0.24 / million tokens | Apache-2.0 weights and custom decision head in first-party `Cloudflare/clef` |
| Clef-flash | Workers AI `@cf/cloudflare/clef-flash`; 9B; multimodal; same hosted context/interface | $0.09 / million tokens | Apache-2.0 first-party weights and head |
| Jev | TypeSafe `POST /v1/systemone`; pin `jev-1.13.0`; text only; 64k total request, with state plus longest question limited to 32k | $0.042 / million tokens; output free | Hosted version can be pinned; no first-party public weights or offline runtime located in this review |

Prices and limits are from current official docs, not launch promotions. Clef/Clef-flash require a Cloudflare account/token for hosted use; Jev's quickstart requires console login and an API key. Actual account entitlement was not checked. [Clef hosted docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clef/), [Flash hosted docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clef-flash/), [Jev models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models), [Jev quickstart](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/quickstart).

For illustration, 1,000 requests of 10,000 billed input tokens each cost $2.40 for Clef, $0.90 for Flash, and $0.42 for Jev at these token rates. This arithmetic excludes other platform charges, retries, labeling, and local hardware. Compare against measured EventEdge call usage before estimating savings.

Clef's model card documents a custom `joint_schema_model`/`systemone` path, tested with Torch 2.11 and Transformers 5.10.2 on one H200. Use that path; the generic Hugging Face text-generation snippets do not establish that the decision head works in Ollama, MLX, or ordinary vLLM serving. Mac, CPU, quantized-head correctness, and VPS capacity were not tested. Raw BF16 parameter storage is roughly 54 GB for 27B and 18 GB for 9B before runtime overhead; those are arithmetic estimates, not hardware requirements. A pinned batch job is preferable to maintaining a new always-on GPU service. [Clef model card](https://huggingface.co/Cloudflare/clef), [Flash model card](https://huggingface.co/Cloudflare/clef-flash).

## Fit against the actual repository

Reviewed local source: `tradingagents/strategies/learning/llm_analyzer.py`, `tradingagents/strategies/trading/portfolio_committee.py`, `portfolio_policy.py`, `risk_gate.py`, `tradingagents/strategies/orchestration/multi_strategy_engine.py`, and README. These establish local design; they are not evidence of live production version or deployment.

The analyzer requests direction, conviction, text rationales, material changes, and sometimes newly inferred tickers. The committee ranks ideas and initially sizes them, then `PortfolioPolicy` constrains both LLM and rule-based outputs and `RiskGate` revalidates at execution. Its prompt currently summarizes the first 20 signals as ticker/direction/score/strategy, caps positions at 10, and includes selected regime/enrichment fields. Merely changing the model cannot recover omitted event evidence. A fair replacement comparison must first use the identical serialized state; richer evidence is a separate experimental arm.

| Responsibility | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Filing/docket relevance, stated catalyst category, direct company exposure among verified candidates | Promising bounded judgments with an explicit insufficient-evidence option |
| Check whether source text supports an analyzer claim | Promising shadow audit; needs independently adjudicated evidence labels |
| Generate rationale, discover arbitrary ticker names, multi-hop regulatory impacts | Keep generative enrichment; a closed option model cannot directly supply these open outputs |
| HTTP retry/status, schema validity, freshness, dates, numeric quality, ledger identity, exposure and cash/margin arithmetic | Keep deterministic; adding semantic inference here creates an unnecessary new failure dependency |
| Trade selection and sizing | Defer; requires EventEdge-specific prospective evidence, portfolio interactions and outcome calibration |
| Causal effect of an event on returns | Not established; bounded causal-answer benchmarks are not identified causal estimates from market data |

TypeSafe recommends atomic questions composed in code. Its current limitations explicitly include unreliable numeric precision and date ordering, long irrelevant state, option-order effects, indirection, and adversarial text. A typed answer can still be semantically wrong; vendor “zero hallucinations” language must not be read as zero decision error. [Jev introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction), [Jev 1.13 limitations](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13).

Jev `confidence` summarizes its probability distribution. For an n-option Choice it is `(max_probability - 1/n)/(1 - 1/n)`, so 0.6 maximum probability across three options yields 0.4 confidence. This is neither probability of trade profit nor EventEdge's current conviction scale. Keep raw distributions in a separate field and estimate thresholds/calibration on held-out domain labels; never wire them into existing short thresholds or position sizes unchanged. [Official confidence definition](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence).

Published evidence supports testing, not automatic adoption. Cloudflare's own benchmark table includes financial entity classification, causal-question answering and forecasting, but no EventEdge net-return evaluation. Flash is not uniformly equivalent to Clef: reported RAGTruth F1 is 35.6 versus 79.4. An independent Jev preprint reports strong selective classification, but shows binary thresholds need domain tuning; another finds inconsistent probabilities across logically related questions. These are recent preprints and do not measure trading profitability. [Cloudflare evaluation table](https://huggingface.co/Cloudflare/clef), [Jev benchmark paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647), [probability-coherence paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33209).

Fine-tuning is premature. Cloudflare's announced service begins with its engineering team; public pricing and a stable self-service workflow were not verified. Jev docs say customer fine-tuning/LoRA is unavailable; domain rules belong in state/questions or a downstream classical model. Domain labels, costs and the evaluation design matter more than training now. TypeSafe says customer requests are not used for training, while enterprise ZDR is a separate offering; do not assume standard-account zero retention. [Cloudflare announcement](https://blog.cloudflare.com/clef-decision-models/), [Jev models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models), [TypeSafe legal](https://docs.typesafe.ai/legal).

## Scoped evaluation proposal — not yet run

1. **Freeze inputs and identity.** Create an isolated evaluation dataset from read-only snapshots or copies, never live ledger writes. Record original generation/configuration, event/source IDs, decision cutoff, publication and actual availability times, content hash, candidate-company verification, governed price provenance, model/revision, prompt/schema, token usage, failures and latency. If contemporaneous source payloads or availability cannot be proved, exclude historical rows from causal/alpha claims and collect future shadow data. Include rejected/non-selected and no-trade candidates to avoid filled-trade selection bias.
2. **First target: semantic evidence, not profits.** Start with one strategy and two atomic questions: whether supplied evidence supports the stated catalyst, and whether the company exposure is direct, indirect, unrelated or insufficiently stated. Build approximately 300–500 distinct events for a feasibility pilot; independently label a stratified subset blind to model and future return. This pilot detects obvious mismatch and estimates labeling burden; it is not enough to validate alpha. Any eventual training/fine-tuning needs a larger point-in-time labeled set and explicit label definitions.
3. **Matched benchmarks.** Compare frozen current Luna enrichment plus committee/policy, deterministic rule-based committee plus the same policy, Clef shadow, pinned Jev shadow, and Flash control. Use equal supplied evidence and fixed candidates. Add a richer-source arm separately to distinguish model benefit from input benefit. Measure semantic macro-F1 and recall at a fixed error/coverage target, Brier/log loss, reliability curves, abstention coverage, disagreements, option-permutation/prompt-injection sensitivity, failure rates, full retries, p50/p95 latency, total tokens/cost and maintenance burden. A no-filter arm measures whether screening discards useful events.
4. **Control leakage and dependence.** Split forward by decision date; group duplicated filings/articles/event clusters and ticker-related observations. Use training then calibration then untouched test windows; purge any training label period crossing a later split and embargo by the longest outcome horizon. Fit weights, calibrators and thresholds only before test. Count one underlying event once; the 16 size/horizon scenario books are dependent observations, not 16 independent samples. Future shadow predictions recorded before outcomes are necessary because historical events may already be in model training data. Version pinning alone does not eliminate that contamination.
5. **Only then examine decisions.** A fixed filter/second-opinion arm uses unchanged exits, deterministic sizing, policy/gates, next-session simulated fills, costs and SPY/BIL benchmarks in separate scratch books. Score paired out-of-sample net returns, turnover, drawdown, tail losses, exposure and rejected-winner opportunity cost; use date/event-cluster block confidence intervals. Return-sign calibration needs explicit horizon/cost labels and realized outcomes, separately from semantic labels. Precommit one primary target/horizon; treat other horizons and strategies as exploratory, with multiple-testing controls. Causal attribution needs an event-study/identification design, not a decision model asking whether the event caused a price move.
6. **Acceptance and stopping rule.** Require a preregistered semantic-quality noninferiority margin, useful coverage, lower measured total operating cost, no new policy bypass, and honest degradation on API failures. Reject the candidate if gains depend on post-hoc threshold tuning, hindsight evidence, hidden exclusions, or continual prompt maintenance. A profitable retrospective slice does not satisfy production promotion: require prospective 30/60/90-session gates and sufficient independent events. Any integrated output-affecting model change requires a fresh generation and Pedro's separate explicit deployment instruction.

This sequence keeps provider reliability work independent and makes a decision-model benefit measurable. No replacement is justified until it survives the same evidence, costs and governance as the existing LLM plus deterministic baseline.
